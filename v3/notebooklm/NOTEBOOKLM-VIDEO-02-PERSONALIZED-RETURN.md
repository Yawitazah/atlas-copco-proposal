# NotebookLM source brief 02: Use What the Prospect Told You

## Video purpose

Show how Goal Pulse Check responses can power a personalized return experience that keeps qualified prospects engaged and moves them toward a commercial next step. This is the second of three short strategy videos.

This video is presented by **Lorenzo White**, **Founder of ZAH Brand Solutions**, a **Growth Marketing Strategy & Systems Architect**, and a candidate for the **Marketing Communications Manager – APE** role.

Suggested duration: 60–90 seconds.

## The business context

According to Lorenzo's account of the interview, Edson needs marketing that does more than generate a lead. It must help convert that lead.

A generic thank-you message or broad equipment ad ignores the context the prospect already supplied. When follow-up feels disconnected from the original need, interest cools and the lead can become another record sitting in the CRM.

The Goal Pulse Check gives the system a better starting point: the prospect's identity, site, application, equipment need, buying-team role, timing, and preferred next step.

## The strategy

### Build a personalized equipment page

The survey responses shape a complete sales page for the prospect. It should feel more like a useful personalized recommendation than a generic product catalog.

The page can include:

- The prospect's name, company, site, and stated goal.
- The relevant equipment starting point and why it may fit the application.
- Strong machine imagery and grounded capability information.
- Clear actions to schedule a site walkthrough, request pricing or a quote, or share the page with the buying team.

The page keeps the prospect focused on the equipment and next step relevant to their stated need.

### Send a “We heard you” email

Show a fictional personalized email preview. A useful subject line is:

**Marcus, we heard what your site needs.**

Marcus is a fictional demonstration prospect. The email should reflect the application, site conditions, capacity need, timeline, and recommended next action captured in the Goal Pulse Check. It should feature the relevant machine, explain why it is being shown, and return the prospect to the personalized equipment page.

The email should make the personalization visible. It should not merely insert a first name into generic copy.

### Use personalized advertising to bring the prospect back

Keep two advertising functions distinct:

- **Prospecting campaigns** introduce Portable Air to relevant people who have not entered the system.
- **Retargeting campaigns** reconnect with eligible people already captured by the system.

Group captured prospects by application, equipment interest, and readiness for a walkthrough, pricing conversation, or quote. Create personalized ad creative that reflects that prospect context.

LinkedIn and YouTube are the primary retargeting examples in Lorenzo's original strategy. Instagram and Facebook mockups can show how the same campaign idea could adapt to other approved placements. Each ad should lead back to the prospect's personalized equipment page or another clear next step.

### Keep the human relationship visible

The personalized page, email, and ads support a named sales professional. They should make it easier for the prospect to re-engage, recognize the representative, and take the next action. They do not replace a sales conversation.

## Conversion meaning

The survey does not end at submit. It powers the messages that bring the prospect back.

Use this central message:

**We heard what you need. Here is the most useful next step.**

The desired response is measurable movement: return to the equipment page, share with the buying team, schedule a walkthrough, request pricing, or request a quote.

## Recommended story sequence

1. Show a prospect completing the Goal Pulse Check.
2. Highlight a few fictional responses: site, application, capacity need, timing, and preferred next step.
3. Show those responses assembling a personalized equipment page.
4. Open the “We heard you” email with the relevant machine and call to action.
5. Show LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, and Facebook creative adapting to the same prospect context.
6. Show the prospect returning to the page and choosing a walkthrough, pricing request, quote request, or share action.
7. End with the assigned sales professional receiving the visible next action.

## Narration direction

- Make personalization specific and observable.
- Use the words **personalized equipment page**, **personalized creative**, **retargeting**, **prospect data**, **conversion**, and **next action** where they clarify the process.
- Avoid vague phrases such as “deliver relevance” without explaining what changes for the prospect.
- Keep the language concise, helpful, and respectful.
- Do not imply that personalization guarantees a sale.

Useful ideas that may be expressed naturally:

- “Use what the prospect told you.”
- “Personalization should change the equipment, message, and next step they see.”
- “The survey powers the return; it does not disappear into a database.”
- “Every message should give the prospect a relevant reason to continue.”

## Visual direction

Make the relationship between input and output easy to see:

**Goal Pulse Check answer → personalized page element → personalized email detail → personalized ad → conversion action.**

Use icons for site, application, equipment, timeline, buying team, email, LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, site walkthrough, pricing, quote, and share. Use one consistent fictional prospect across the sequence so the personalization feels connected.

## Required factual boundaries

- This is Lorenzo's proposed strategy, not an active Atlas Copco campaign.
- The prospect, responses, email, equipment page, and advertisements are fictional demonstrations.
- Do not state that Atlas Copco currently lacks personalized email, retargeting, or sales pages.
- No live email, social-ad account, audience, pixel, CRM, or C4C connection is active in the prototype.
- Retargeting requires consent, appropriate notice, identity matching, minimum audience size, platform eligibility, and approved data use.
- Use only relevant, non-sensitive business context supplied or lawfully available for personalization.
- Equipment recommendations are starting points and require application and engineering review.
- Walkthrough, pricing, and quote actions shown in the prototype are requests, not confirmed outcomes.
- Do not guarantee engagement, conversion, revenue, or campaign performance.
